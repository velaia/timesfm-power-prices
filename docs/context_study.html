Does more history improve tomorrow’s price forecast?

Only a little. At quarter-hour resolution TimesFM 3 stops improving at about 112 days of context. Hourly steps allow up to 640 days of history, but they lose more in quarter-hour detail than the extra history wins back. The bigger finding is the full-year backtest. Over 365 days the error is lower than the 90-day test suggested: 12.4 EUR/MWh with all covariates and 14.4 with weather and calendar only.

Hourly steps with 480 days of history still lose to 15-minute steps with 56

At 15-minute resolution the gain flattens after 56 days and stops at 112. The 160-day maximum scores 13.40, against 13.22 at 112 days. Coarser steps do keep improving with more history. Hourly forecasts fall from 16.04 at 112 days to 14.18 at 480, and half-hourly ones from 15.13 to 14.58 at 320. They start too far behind to catch up. One cheap gain did show up: a blend of 75% of the current forecast and 25% of the 480-day hourly one scores 12.98 (weather only: 15.66). That weight was picked on these same 90 days, so it should be confirmed on the full year before it's used.

Hourly steps can’t draw the quarter-hour pattern

Since October 2025 the auction clears every quarter hour, and prices within an hour often move in a saw-tooth. Even a perfect hourly forecast, spread back to quarter hours, would be off by 5.6 EUR/MWh on average, or 9.0 if each hour is held flat. That is a large share of the 13.2 the 15-minute model scores. Half-hourly steps lose less (3.3), which is why they come closer.

Over a full year the model does better than the 90-day test showed

The original test covered July to early October, a solar-heavy stretch that turned out to be harder than average. Over the 365 forecast days from 2025-10-06 to 2026-10-05, all covariates score 12.4 and weather + calendar 14.4, against 30.2 for same as yesterday. Winter is the easiest season, at 10.8 from October to March. The error follows price volatility, and June and September are the hardest months. Only the cheapest-3-hour hit rate drops, to 74% and 66%. The misses are mostly on flat winter days, where several 3-hour windows cost almost the same. Using the forecast’s window costs only 0.9 EUR/MWh more than the true cheapest one on average, against 5.8 for same as yesterday.

How this was run

  • Model: google/timesfm-3.0-pytorch, zero-shot, on an M1 Max GPU (MPS), with the repo’s settings (symmetric averaging on, make_positive off).
  • Every forecast is issued as of 10:00 on D-1 and predicts every quarter hour of D (92, 96 or 100 on DST days).
  • Context limit: TimesFM 3 accepts at most 15,360 steps (_MAX_CONTEXT_LENGTH). That is 160 days of quarter hours, 320 days of half hours or 640 days of hours. Longer inputs are cut without a warning.
  • Coarse runs: prices and covariates are averaged into UTC half hours or hours and forecast at that resolution. The forecast is then spread back to quarter hours, either held flat or interpolated through the bucket centres, and scored against the real 15-minute prices.
  • Longer contexts need older data, so the dataset was rebuilt from 2024-03-01, the first date with complete Open-Meteo previous-day runs (100 m wind starts in February 2024). Before 2025-10-01 the market cleared hourly, so those prices are hourly values repeated four times.
  • In the year-long run, forecast days before about 2026-01-21 have context that partly predates 15-minute trading. That is what a live run would have seen at the time.
  • The TSO caveat from the README still applies. The all-covariates numbers use SMARD’s final TSO forecasts, which have so far appeared on SMARD only after the auction. Weather + calendar is the setup that is achievable live today.
  • Reproduce: the commands are in docs/RESULTS.md, and the code is in experiments/context_study.
